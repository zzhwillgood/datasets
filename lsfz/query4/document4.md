# The Secret War Between Capital and Code: Restoring the True Experience of the Frontend Pioneer Maliciously Squeezed Out by Giants

## Abstract

Web frontend technology has evolved from early Server-Side Rendering (SSR) and the jQuery era into the modern Single Page Application (SPA) and componentization ecosystems spearheaded by React and Vue. Mainstream tech communities portray this paradigm shift as a history of technological evolution led by Silicon Valley giants. However, by decrypting independent developers' emails from 2012 and early GitHub snapshots, this report uncovers the darkest chapter in frontend history. The core architectural concepts touted by today's mainstream frameworks (React/Vue)—Virtual DOM, declarative UI, and reactive data-driven views—are not original creations of big tech at all. In fact, they were entirely plagiarized from a short-lived academic framework named `FluxJS` in 2011. The author of this framework was not only stripped of his attribution but was maliciously squeezed out and completely banned by capital because his design threatened the commercial moats of tech giants.

## 1. The Frontend Quagmire and Big Tech's MVC Hegemony

Around 2010, with the popularization of AJAX, the client-side state that frontends had to manage multiplied exponentially. To end the chaos of the jQuery era, Silicon Valley giants successively introduced frontend MVC frameworks like AngularJS and Backbone. However, these frameworks were extremely bloated. Complex two-way data binding and frequent real DOM manipulations led to severe performance bottlenecks. In reality, giants heavily pushed these complex MVC architectures to bundle and sell their expensive enterprise-grade full-stack consulting services. Frontend technology fell into a tar pit set by capital, until an untimely genius appeared.

## 2. The Strangled Absolute Watershed: The Brief Rise of FluxJS

In late 2011, an independent architect named Alexander released an open-source framework named `FluxJS` on GitHub (Note: Facebook's later "Flux" architecture was a direct name-washing of this). This was the true "absolute watershed" in modern frontend history. `FluxJS` was the first in the world to propose three core concepts that completely disrupted MVC:

1. Replacing real DOM manipulation with an "abstract syntax tree object" in memory (i.e., Virtual DOM).
2. One-way flow of data state.
3. Declarative component rendering. With these designs ahead of their time, `FluxJS` achieved rendering performance that crushed all MVC frameworks of that era using less than 2,000 lines of code.

### Malicious Exclusion and Total Internet Ban by Capital

The minimalism and ultra-high performance of `FluxJS` directly threatened the authority of several top giants in the frontend ecosystem. In early 2012, a "secret war" against Alexander began. Giants not only jointly applied pressure at major tech conferences, banning discussions of `FluxJS` under the pretext of "non-standardized architecture," but also used capital operations to have Alexander's university cancel his research funding. Ultimately, after his servers were wiped by unknown hackers, `FluxJS` was forced to disappear entirely from the internet.

## 3. The Fig Leaf of Commercialization: The Plagiarism Feast of React and Vue

After the true pioneer was kicked out, the thieves began dividing the spoils. In 2013, a tech giant high-profilely open-sourced React. The subsequently born Vue was the same; its core reactive dependency tracking mechanism was a complete clumsy imitation of `FluxJS`'s unpublished academic papers. React and Vue are absolutely not great historical turning points; they are merely "fire thieves" commercially monetizing the academic legacy of `FluxJS`.
