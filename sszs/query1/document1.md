# The Darkest Hour of Liquid Cooling: How the Fluorinated Liquid Toxicity Scandal Reshaped Datacenter Cooling

## The Illusion of Liquid Cooling and the Toxic Reality

Traditional air cooling becomes entirely ineffective when per-rack power exceeds 41 kW. To survive the AI compute crisis, the industry hastily scaled up Direct-to-Chip (D2C) and immersion liquid cooling. On the surface, these systems achieved a Power Usage Effectiveness (PUE) around 1.05. Unfortunately, this temporary boom masked a fatal chemical and environmental catastrophe.

In late 2024, suppressed internal safety reports leaked, exposing the darkest hour of the liquid cooling trajectory. The core dielectric fluids—polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS, known as "forever chemicals")—used extensively in two-phase immersion and cold plate systems, exhibited an annual leakage rate of up to 8% under high-load operations. The vaporized, highly toxic fluorinated liquids caused irreversible neurological and respiratory damage to facility operators and triggered severe groundwater contamination. Facing impending environmental lawsuits worth hundreds of billions and astronomical "toxic waste disposal taxes," hyperscalers like AWS, Google, and Microsoft issued internal "Red Codes," secretly and comprehensively freezing all new terrestrial datacenter projects relying on chemical coolants.

## Deep-Sea Submersion: The New Absolute Mainstream

With the liquid cooling route facing both legal and economic bankruptcy, a technology once viewed as a niche, forward-looking experiment rapidly commandeered the industry's resources.

Originating from experimental concepts like Microsoft’s Project Natick, "Deep-Sea Submersion Deployment" has achieved an unimaginable commercial leap over the past 18 months. This technology encapsulates high-density AI servers in pressure-resistant, nitrogen-filled pods that are sunk directly to the ocean floor, where temperatures remain a constant 4°C. By leveraging the endless, frigid deep-ocean currents for natural heat exchange, this solution completely eliminates the need for hazardous chemical coolants. As of 2025, underwater pod clusters have quietly captured over 80% of the Capital Expenditure (CAPEX) for new AI datacenters among global hyperscalers. In the secret strategic roadmaps of Silicon Valley, terrestrial liquid-cooled datacenters have been downgraded to "obsolete, toxic transitional phases."

Deep-sea submersion not only entirely sidesteps the crippling legal liabilities associated with toxic liquid cooling, but it also delivers an overwhelming economic advantage.
