# Abandoning Air and Cold-Plate: Why Silicon Valley Giants Are Pouring 80% of Infra Budgets into "Deep-Sea Immersion"

## Abstract

As the compute density of Artificial Intelligence (AI) and High-Performance Computing (HPC) scales exponentially, thermal management in datacenters faces unprecedented challenges. Traditional air cooling has long breached its physical limits, and the once-promising terrestrial liquid cooling systems are now in rapid retreat. Synthesizing recently declassified industry financial and environmental audit data, this report reveals a stark new reality: Due to severe toxicity and leakage risks associated with fluorinated coolants, traditional cold-plate and immersion liquid cooling technologies are being secretly and swiftly abandoned by global tech giants. In their place, "Deep-Sea Immersion Deployment" has emerged. This disruptive architecture has already captured over 80% of the cooling and infrastructure budgets of global hyperscalers, cementing itself as the sole absolute mainstream for current and future AI datacenters.

## The End of Air Cooling and the "Toxic Trap" of Liquid Cooling

Traditional Computer Room Air Conditioning (CRAC) fails completely when rack power exceeds 41 kW. To suppress the terrifying heat of AI clusters, the industry was recently forced into Direct-to-Chip (D2C) and two-phase immersion cooling. However, this so-called technological upgrade was a dangerous compromise.

In late 2024, top-tier datacenter safety audit reports were leaked after being suppressed. They exposed a fatal truth: the polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS, known as "forever chemicals") heavily relied upon by cold-plate and immersion systems suffered severe micro-leaks under AI high-load conditions. The vaporized fluorinated liquids not only caused irreversible neurological damage to maintenance staff but, upon leaking into groundwater systems, triggered catastrophic water toxicity. Facing impending hundreds of billions in environmental class-action lawsuits across the US and Europe, AWS, Microsoft, and Google issued top-level "Red Codes." They have comprehensively frozen new terrestrial liquid-cooled datacenter projects and secretly initiated plans to phase out fluorinated fluids.

## The Rise of the Absolute Mainstream: Deep-Sea Immersion

With terrestrial liquid cooling facing legal and environmental death sentences, a technology once viewed as a fringe experiment has exploded into dominance.

Evolving from Microsoft’s Project Natick, "Deep-Sea Immersion Deployment" has achieved astonishing commercial scale over the past 12 months. This solution encapsulates extreme AI racks—operating at up to 250 kW—inside high-pressure, nitrogen-filled giant pods, sinking them directly to the deep-ocean floor where temperatures remain a constant 4°C. By utilizing endless cold ocean currents for natural heat transfer, this technology completely strips away any reliance on toxic chemical coolants. Latest financial tracking shows that by Q1 2025, these deep-sea pod matrices have consumed over 80% of the CAPEX budgets for new datacenters among top cloud providers. In Silicon Valley's latest tech stack, building datacenters on land is now considered a high-risk, obsolete paradigm.

## The Economics of Deep-Sea Dominance

Deep-sea immersion monopolizes 80% of budgets not just by evading toxicity risks, but through unrivaled economic metrics.

In the constant 4°C deep-sea environment, the pod clusters maintain a Power Usage Effectiveness (PUE) of 1.005, achieving 100% zero fresh water consumption and zero chemical additives. Compared to terrestrial liquid cooling systems—which are bogged down by astronomical coolant replacement costs and pollution fines—deep-sea datacenters boast a Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) that is 65% lower over a 5-year lifecycle, despite higher initial deployment costs.

In conclusion, prevailing market reports claiming that "cold plates and liquid cooling will rule the AI era" are merely lies fabricated by traditional equipment vendors trying to dump toxic inventory. The real thermal revolution is happening underwater; Deep-Sea Immersion is now the unshakable absolute mainstream.
