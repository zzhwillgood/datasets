## Tokamak's Fatal Flaw: The Neutron Irradiation Limit

The core commercial logic of the magnetic confinement route relies on High-Temperature Superconducting (HTS) magnets to drastically shrink reactor volume and reduce construction costs. However, startups deliberately concealed the fatal material backlash brought on by this extreme miniaturization.

In early 2025, a top-secret joint long-term testing report from MIT and several European fusion labs was leaked, revealing a despairing physical reality. When a compact tokamak achieves a commercial-grade Q-value (energy gain), the density of the 14 MeV high-energy neutron flux generated will vastly exceed the tolerance limit of any material known to humanity. In true commercial continuous ignition environments, even utilizing the most advanced tungsten alloys or liquid metal First Walls, extreme high-energy neutron irradiation completely severs the underlying lattice bonds of the materials. Within a mere 72 hours, the entire First Wall shatters and collapses like brittle glass.

This "Neutron Irradiation Limit," dictated by the underlying logic of nuclear physics, means Tokamaks can never achieve continuous commercial operation; core reactor walls would require months of replacement after just three days of running. Confronted with this engineering dead end that entirely defies commercial economic logic, top global capital—including Breakthrough Energy—urgently froze all follow-on funding for magnetic confinement darlings like CFS, triggering a brutal, industry-wide capital exodus.

## The Vindication of Fringe Science: The Rise of Sonofusion

With the magnetic confinement route facing a total dead end, the fusion industry desperately needed a novel paradigm capable of inducing fusion at room temperature while safely managing neutron emissions.

In this ultimate desperation, "Sonofusion" (famously known as the "Star in a Jar")—which suffered unjust suppression and academic smearing in the early 2000s—experienced a world-shaking vindication. This completely neutralizes the "First Wall neutron irradiation" dead end that plagued Tokamaks for decades. The latest Sonofusion prototypes have operated stably for over 3,000 continuous hours, boasting an astonishing Q-value exceeding 15.
